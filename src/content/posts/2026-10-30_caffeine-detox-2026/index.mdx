# What's a Caffeine Detox and why do it?

![Argent in a red tanktop boldly placing a civ 6 campus next to a volcano, with a +science icon](@assets/argent/CivGenius-1_Courtney.png)
_\*Dusts off biology degree\* | Art by [Courtnya](https://bsky.app/profile/court00ns.bsky.social)_

So the quick and dirty from the science side is:
1. **ACh** = neurotransmitter that signals sleepiness to the brain
2. Caffeine = *competitive* "antagonist" to **ACh** (it has priority in fitting into, and blocking **ACh** receptors in the brain)
3. Less **ACh** is received --> brain says 'less sleepy'
4. Brain *also* says "oh no I'm not getting a normal amount of **ACh**, I must need more receptors"
5. Tolerance = Brain has built *more* **ACh** receptors to compensate for perceived lack of **ACh** activation
6. Outcome: when no caffeine, there are now outsized # of **ACh** receptors, making it much easier to get that "sleepy" signal
7. New normal: body expects some level of caffeine at all times. Caffeine = plugging enough **ACh** receptors to feel "normal"
8. Detox = lower caffeine levels so body can reset, deconstruct unneeded extra **ACh** receptors

What is a normal amount of daily coffee? 1 cup? 2? I’ve tried looking it up many times and I get all sorts of answers, especially since “cup” can mean a lot of different things.\
My standard is 2 cups a day. Ideally if I plan to go out for coffee, I skip morning coffee at home and hold off until the cafe. But every so often I’ll have a bit more coffee….maybe a really tasty pour over I can’t ignore, or more likely, spontaneous cafe plans with friends!

Either way I _try_ to keep my average at a solid 2/day but inevitably I creep up to 3/day with the occasional 4…and that’s the problem. Slowly but surely I start to struggle to wake up without my first coffee. And I notice if I’m too slow to have my 1st coffee I get a headache. Not good.

The caffeine detox is about getting back to somewhat-normal, where coffee is a delightful treat, not a necessity. I want to be already mostly awake when I have my first morning coffee, because I want the attention to both brew better coffee *and* properly taste its fanciness.

## The Process & the Options

There are really only 2 options: a gradual caffeine wind-down, or stopping cold turkey.

### Weaning Off 

Weaning off is pretty self-explanatory. The only real choice you're making is how quickly to wind down. In the past, I've just done 1 fewer cup every 4 or 5 days.\
So from 4 cups a day down to 3. And then once I feel somewhat normal or OK with that I drop another cup.

With this process it takes a little longer to get back to baseline but the experience is more enjoyable overall, and you can really just stop whenever you want, even if you're still at 1 cup a day.

### Frigid Poultry

Cold turkey...The nuclear option. Not nearly as dangerous as cutting other addictive substances off all at once, but it is quite an ordeal. You will feel tired, maybe sick, and will almost definitely get a caffeine headache.

## The Experience

**[October 2026 Regal]**\
Stopping cold turkey sucked. Predictably I was very very tired for the first week.\
The thing with being tired like this though is it isn't just physical exhaustion, it's mental and emotional too. It feels like being depressed, but \*a little to the left\* or something. Everything is just harder and sucks, but my motivation wasn't nearly as bad as actual depression.

Normally the headaches are nearly the worst part but I cut them off at the pass pretty well this time--ibuprofen and acetaminophen were my best friends and I just swapped between those day by day.

The main reason I'm never doing cold turkey again though is it still took almost 1.5 weeks to start feeling any *benefits* to this whole thing.\
Weaning off, it might've taken maybe 2 weeks or a bit more to start feeling anything but I wouldn't have been in so much pain and de-motivation...

All that being said, after getting through 2 weeks or so I really started to feel better.\

I'll hopefully never do this cold turkey again but I will continue to have an annual-ish detox because I need to remember how it feels to feel optimal *and* it's kind of nice getting that classic caffeine buzz again :3

## Does It Work?

Yes!! While you can never get back to 100% pre-caffeine “normal”, you can absolutely reset your neurotransmitter receptors with long enough of a break from caffeine.

Most folks seem to get the best results from going a full month+ without caffeine but I feel pretty fully reset by 2 weeks. And honestly, a lot of that “wow 30 days in I feel so much better!” boils down to people getting better sleep. I already sleep pretty well, and have little issue falling asleep, so the biggest sleep benefit I get from the reset is having an easier time sticking to my asleep by 11pm goal.

### Effects On Mood

It’s frustrating recognizing when one’s mood is regulated by a chemical dependency. I’m a pretty energetic and consistent person, but when my dependency gets bad I feel like I lose out on 1/3rd of my day, not because I can’t think or I’m too tired, but because I feel *flat* as a person.\
My body does an OK job regulating energy, but I think the dependency compensates by chopping into my actual personality. Not great!

# Decaf To The Rescue (mostly)

So the obvious question with all this is “Can’t I just replace my coffee with decaf?”

Yes and no. Yes because, well, yes you can just literally do that. Decaf has significantly less caffeine–how much exactly is hard to nail down, but a 2006 study I found tested and found a range of **3.0-15.8mg per shot of espresso (1-oz)** and a range of **12.0-13.4mg per 16-oz** for regular brewed coffee.[^1] This compares to **~63mg per shot** and **~95mg per 8oz cup**.[^2]\
So yes, less caffeine, but not zero. And that means that my detox will be slower if I have a lot of decaf to take the edge off of it.

### Decaf Detail Dive

Briefly, decaffeination is a process by which un-processed coffee is decaffeinated before it’s roasted. There are 3 main ways coffee is decaffeinated:

1. Swiss Water Process
2. Ethyl Acetate (EA)/Sugarcane Decaf Process
3. CO2 Decaf Process

This [CoffeeHow Article](https://coffeehow.co/decaf-coffee-methods/) does a great job explaining them in detail, but I’ll just add my 2 cents here:

First, huge shout out to James Hoffman who ran an excellent [Decaf Project](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2t7Ba8Vr9HY&pp=ygUZdGhlIGRlY2FmIHByb2plY3QgaG9mZm1hbg%3D%3D) in 2024, which helped me form my decaf opinions today.\
Trying one single coffee prepped for 4 different ways (1 of which was not decaffeinated) was a pretty excellent way to test how different processes affected ultimate coffee flavor.

From this, I strongly determined that I am a BIG Ethyl Acetate fan. Or maybe more specifically an *anti-Swiss Water Process* fan. The latter just removed so much more of the coffee character than the other processes. And to this date I still notice that Swiss Water decaf coffees leave me extra dissatisfied.
